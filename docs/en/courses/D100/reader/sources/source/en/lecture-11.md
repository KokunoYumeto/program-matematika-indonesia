# Lecture 11: Hilbert's Nullstellensatz and coordinate rings {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11}

## Hilbert's Nullstellensatz - geometric version {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-s01}

We now prove the geometric version of Hilbert's Nullstellensatz. For an
algebraically closed field, this theorem establishes a one-to-one
relationship between affine algebraic sets in affine space $\mathbb A_K^n$
and radical ideals in the polynomial ring.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affiner Raum/Hilbertscher Nullstellensatz (geometrisch)/Algebraisch abgeschlossen/Fakt -->

### Theorem: the geometric Hilbert Nullstellensatz {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-thm-01}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and let

$$
V=V(\mathfrak a)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n
$$

be an affine algebraic set described by the ideal $\mathfrak a$. Let

$$
F\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]
$$

be a polynomial vanishing on $V$. Then $F$ belongs to the radical of
$\mathfrak a$; that is, there is an $r\in\mathbb N$ with

$$
F^r\in\mathfrak a.
$$

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-thm-01-proof}

Suppose that $F$ does not belong to the radical of $\mathfrak a$. By
Theorem 10.9, there is a maximal ideal

$$
\mathfrak m\subset K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]
$$

with $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak m$ and $F\notin\mathfrak m$. By
Theorem 10.10,

$$
K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\mathfrak m=K,
$$

and hence

$$
\mathfrak m=(X_1-a_1,\ldots,X_n-a_n)
$$

for some $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in K$. The property $F\notin\mathfrak m$ means
that $F$ is nonzero in the corresponding residue field, namely

$$
F(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\ne0.
$$

But since $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak m$, the point
$(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ belongs to $V$. By assumption, $F$ must vanish at that
point. This is a contradiction.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affiner Raum/Algebraisch abgeschlossen/Korrespondenz zwischen affin algebraischen Mengen und Radikalen/Fakt -->

### Theorem: correspondence between algebraic sets and radical ideals {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-thm-02}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field, with polynomial ring
$K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ and affine space $\mathbb A_K^n$. There is a natural
correspondence between affine algebraic sets in $\mathbb A_K^n$ and
radical ideals in $K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$.

In this correspondence, a radical ideal is sent to its zero locus, and an
affine algebraic set is sent to its vanishing ideal.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-thm-02-proof}

Let $V\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n$ be affine algebraic. By part (3) of Lemma 3.8,

$$
V=V(\operatorname{Id}(V)).
$$

For a radical ideal $I\subseteq K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, part (2) of the same
lemma gives the inclusion

$$
I\subseteq\operatorname{Id}(V(I)).
$$

The reverse inclusion,

$$
\operatorname{Id}(V(I))\subseteq I,
$$

is the content of Hilbert's Nullstellensatz.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affiner Raum/Algebraisch abgeschlossener Körper/D(f i) überdeckt/Erzeugt Einheitsideal/Fakt -->

### Corollary: covers by principal open sets {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-01}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and let

$$
F_i\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n],\qquad i\in I,
$$

be polynomials such that

$$
\mathbb A_K^n=\bigcup_{i\in I}D(F_i).
$$

Then the $F_i$ generate the unit ideal in $K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-01-proof}

Let $\mathfrak b$ be the ideal generated by all the $F_i$. The assumption
says that

$$
\bigcap_{i\in I}V(F_i)=V(\mathfrak b)
$$

is empty. Thus $V(\mathfrak b)\subseteq V(1)$. By Hilbert's Nullstellensatz,
a power of $1$, hence $1$ itself, belongs to $\mathfrak b$. Therefore
$\mathfrak b$ is the unit ideal.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affine Varietäten/Algebraisch abgeschlossener Körper/Verschwindungsideal vom Durchschnitt/Fakt -->

### Corollary: the vanishing ideal of an intersection {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-02}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and $V_1,V_2$ two affine
algebraic sets in $\mathbb A_K^n$. Then

$$
\operatorname{Id}(V_1\cap V_2)
=\operatorname{rad}\!\left(\operatorname{Id}(V_1)+
\operatorname{Id}(V_2)\right).
$$

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-02-proof}

Let

$$
\mathfrak a_1=\operatorname{Id}(V_1),\qquad
\mathfrak a_2=\operatorname{Id}(V_2).
$$

The statement follows from

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak a_1+\mathfrak a_2)
&=\operatorname{Id}\!\left(V(\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak a_1+
\mathfrak a_2))\right)\\
&=\operatorname{Id}(V(\mathfrak a_1+\mathfrak a_2))\\
&=\operatorname{Id}(V(\mathfrak a_1)\cap V(\mathfrak a_2)),
\end{aligned}
$$

where the first equality comes from Theorem 11.1.

These properties also fail without the assumption that the field is
algebraically closed, as the following example shows.

### Example: two disjoint real quadrics {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-ex-01}

We consider the two algebraic curves

$$
V_1=V(X^2+Y^2-2),\qquad
V_2=V(X^2+2Y^2-1)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^2.
$$

For $K=\mathbb R$, both are irreducible quadrics. Their intersection is
described by the ideal

$$
(X^2+Y^2-2,X^2+2Y^2-1)=(Y^2+1,X^2-3).
$$

Since the polynomial $Y^2+1$ has no real zero,

$$
V_1\cap V_2=\varnothing.
$$

The vanishing ideal of the empty intersection is of course the unit ideal,
whereas the sum of the two vanishing ideals is not the unit ideal.

## The coordinate ring of an affine algebraic set {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-s02}

Let $V\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n$ be an affine algebraic set with vanishing
ideal $\operatorname{Id}(V)$. Every polynomial

$$
F\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]
$$

defines a function on affine space and thus induces a function on the subset $V$:

$$
\begin{matrix}
\mathbb A_K^n&\xrightarrow{F}&K\\
\uparrow&\nearrow&\\
V&&
\end{matrix}
$$

By Definition 3.4, an element of the vanishing ideal induces the zero
function on $V$. Two polynomials $G,H\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ whose
difference belongs to the vanishing ideal induce the same function on $V$.
It is therefore natural to regard the quotient ring

$$
K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\operatorname{Id}(V)
$$

as the ring of polynomial (or algebraic) functions on $V$.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affine-algebraische Mengen/Koordinatenring/Definition -->

### Definition: coordinate ring {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-def-01}

For an affine algebraic set

$$
V\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n
$$

with vanishing ideal $\operatorname{Id}(V)$, the ring

$$
R(V)=K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\operatorname{Id}(V)
$$

is called the *coordinate ring* of $V$.

This notion is not entirely unproblematic, especially when $K$ is not
algebraically closed; see the examples below. We first record some
elementary properties.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affine-algebraische Mengen/Koordinatenring/Grundeigenschaften/Fakt -->

### Proposition: basic properties of coordinate rings {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-prop-01}

Let $V\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n$ be an affine algebraic set and

$$
R=K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\operatorname{Id}(V)
$$

its coordinate ring. Then the following statements hold.

1. $R$ is reduced.
2. $V=\varnothing$ if and only if $R$ is the zero ring.
3. $V$ is irreducible if and only if $R$ is an integral domain.
4. $V$ consists of a single point if and only if $R=K$.
5. If $K$ is algebraically closed and $V=V(\mathfrak a)$, then

   $$
   R=K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak a).
   $$

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-prop-01-proof}

Let $I=\operatorname{Id}(V)$ be the vanishing ideal of $V$.

1. This follows from Lemma 3.14 and Exercise 3.13.
2. The equality $V=\varnothing$ is equivalent to $1\in I$, which in turn
   is equivalent to $R=0$.
3. This follows from Lemma 4.3 and Exercise 4.17.
4. Let $V=\{P\}$ with $P=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$. Then

   $$
   I=(X_1-a_1,\ldots,X_n-a_n)
   $$

   and its coordinate ring is

   $$
   K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/(X_1-a_1,\ldots,X_n-a_n)\cong K.
   $$

   Conversely, if the coordinate ring is $K$, the corresponding quotient
   homomorphism must be an evaluation homomorphism $X_i\mapsto a_i$.
   The vanishing ideal of $V$ must be a point ideal, and
   $P=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in V$. If there were another point $Q\in V$,
   $Q\ne P$, we would obtain a contradiction, because not all the
   $X_i-a_i$ vanish at $Q$.
5. If $K$ is algebraically closed, Hilbert's Nullstellensatz gives

   $$
   \operatorname{Id}(V)=\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak a).
   $$

### Theorem: the coordinate ring of affine space over an infinite field {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-thm-03}

Let $K$ be an infinite field. The vanishing ideal of affine space
$\mathbb A_K^n$ is the zero ideal, and its coordinate ring is the
polynomial ring $K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-thm-03-proof}

We prove the statement by induction on the number of variables. For $n=1$,
it follows from the fact that a polynomial of degree $d$ has at most $d$ roots.

For the induction step, let

$$
F\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]
$$

be a polynomial vanishing at every point of $\mathbb A_K^n=K^n$. Write $F$ as

$$
F=P_dX_n^d+P_{d-1}X_n^{d-1}+\cdots+P_1X_n+P_0,
$$

with

$$
P_d,\ldots,P_0\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_{n-1}].
$$

We must show that $F=0$, which is equivalent to $P_i=0$ for every
$i=0,\ldots,d$. Suppose, without loss of generality, that $P_d$ is not
the zero polynomial. By the induction hypothesis, $P_d$ is not the zero
function either. Thus there is a point $(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})$ with

$$
P_d(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})\ne0.
$$

Consequently, $F(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree
$d$ in the single variable $X_n$. By the one-variable case, it cannot be
the zero function, a contradiction.

> $P_1X_0$. The context of expansion in the variable $X_n$ requires
> $P_1X_n$; this edition uses the consistent index and preserves the source

### Example: finite fields {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-ex-02}

Theorem 11.8 is false for finite fields. Over a finite field, affine space
consists of only finitely many points, and many polynomials vanish at all
these points. Typical examples are the polynomials

$$
X_i^q-X_i,
$$

where $q$ is the number of elements of the field.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affin-algebraische Mengen/Reelle affine Ebene/X^2+Y^2/Verschwindungsideal und Koordinatenring/Beispiel -->

### Example: quotient rings and coordinate rings can differ {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-ex-03}

Let

$$
R=\mathbb R[X,Y]/(X^2+Y^2).
$$

Since squares of real numbers are never negative, the zero locus of
$X^2+Y^2$ consists only of the origin:

$$
V(X^2+Y^2)=\{(0,0)\}.
$$

Its vanishing ideal is the maximal ideal $(X,Y)$, so the corresponding
coordinate ring is

$$
\mathbb R[X,Y]/(X,Y)\cong\mathbb R.
$$

Thus the coordinate ring can be very different from the initial quotient
ring whose ideal was used to define the zero locus.

## Hilbert's Nullstellensatz for affine algebraic sets {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-s03}

Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, as formulated for affine space and the
polynomial ring, holds correspondingly for every $V(\mathfrak a)$ and the
quotient ring $K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\mathfrak a$.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affine Varietäten/Hilbertscher Nullstellensatz (geometrisch)/Algebraisch abgeschlossen/Fakt -->

### Corollary: the Nullstellensatz on an affine algebraic set {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-03}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and let

$$
R=K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\mathfrak a
$$

be a $K$-algebra of finite type with zero locus

$$
V=V(\mathfrak a)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n.
$$

Let $\mathfrak b$ be an ideal in $R$, and $F\in R$ an element vanishing
on $V(\mathfrak b)\subseteq V$. Then there is an $r\in\mathbb N$ with

$$
F^r\in\mathfrak b
$$

in $R$.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-03-proof}

The vanishing condition $V(\mathfrak b)\subseteq V(F)$ inside
$V=V(\mathfrak a)$, translated back into affine space, says that

$$
V(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b)
=V(\mathfrak a)\cap V(\mathfrak b)
\subseteq V(F),
$$

where $F$ is now a representative polynomial in $K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ and
$\mathfrak b$ is the inverse-image ideal in that ring. By Hilbert's
Nullstellensatz for affine space, there is an $r\in\mathbb N$ with

$$
F^r\in\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b.
$$

Modulo $\mathfrak a$, this says precisely that $F^r\in\mathfrak b$ in $R$.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affine Varietäten/Algebraisch abgeschlossener Körper/D(f i) überdeckt/Erzeugt Einheitsideal/Fakt -->

### Corollary: principal open covers of an affine algebraic set {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-04}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and
$V=V(\mathfrak a)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n$ an affine algebraic set described
by the ideal $\mathfrak a$. Let

$$
F_i\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n],\qquad i\in I,
$$

be such that

$$
V=\bigcup_{i\in I}D(F_i).
$$

Then the classes of all the $F_i$ generate the unit ideal in
$K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\mathfrak a$.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-04-proof}

Let $\mathfrak b$ be the ideal in $K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\mathfrak a$
generated by all the $F_i$, $i\in I$. The assumption says that

$$
V(\mathfrak b)=\bigcap_{i\in I}V(F_i)
$$

is empty on $V$. Since $V(1)$ is also empty, $V(1)\subseteq V(\mathfrak b)$.
By Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, a power of $1$, hence $1$ itself, belongs
to $\mathfrak b$.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affine Varietäten/Algebraisch abgeschlossener Körper/Einheit auf Nullstellenmenge und im Restklassenring/Fakt -->

### Corollary: a polynomial with no zeros is a unit {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-05}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and
$V=V(\mathfrak a)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n$ an affine algebraic set described
by the ideal $\mathfrak a$. If $F\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ has no zero on
$V$, then its class is a unit in the quotient ring

$$
K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/\mathfrak a.
$$

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l11-cor-05-proof}

This is a special case of Corollary 11.12.

In Example 11.5, the function $X^2+2Y^2-1$ has no zero on the real zero
locus $V(X^2+Y^2-2)$, so its value is a unit at every point of that locus.
However, it is not a unit in the coordinate ring

$$
\mathbb R[X,Y]/(X^2+Y^2-2).
$$
