### Exercise 17.20 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w17-ex-20}

Let $M$ be a square $r\times r$ matrix with entries in $\mathbb N$, with
associated monoid map and map of spectra

$$
\varphi:
K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(K[\mathbb N^r])
\longrightarrow
K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(K[\mathbb N^r]),
$$

where $K$ is an infinite field. Show that

$$
\det M\ne0
$$

if and only if the image of $\varphi$ contains a nonempty open subset of
$K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(K[\mathbb N^r])$.

**Edition note:** this is the intended meaning of the source's phrase
“maps surjectively onto an open set”. The stated assumption that $K$ is
infinite is insufficient: for $K=\mathbb R$ and $M=(2)$, the image of
$x\mapsto x^2$ contains no nonempty Zariski-open subset of the line.
The assertion holds if $K$ is algebraically closed.

The next exercise uses the following definition. A *filter* $F$ in a
commutative monoid $M$ is a submonoid that is also *closed under taking
divisors*: if $f\in F$ and $g\mid f$, then $g\in F$.

### Exercise 17.22 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w17-ex-22}

Let $M$ be a commutative monoid and let $f\in M$. Consider the set

$$
M_f
=\{m-nf\mid m\in M,\ n\in\mathbb N\}/\!\sim,
$$

where

$$
m-nf\sim m'-n'f
$$

if and only if there is a $k\in\mathbb N$ such that

$$
m+n'f+kf=m'+nf+kf
$$

in $M$.

1. Show that $\sim$ is an equivalence relation.
2. Define a monoid structure on $M_f$.
3. Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $T^f\in R[M]$ be the monomial
   corresponding to $f$. Show that

   $$
   R[M_f]\cong R[M]_{T^f}.
   $$

### Exercise 17.39 (4 points) {#br-ak-2025-2026-w17-ex-39}

Let $K$ be a field and let $G$ be a group. Consider the group ring $K[G]$,
and let $M$ be a $K[G]$-module. Show that:

1. $M$ is nothing other than a $K$-vector space $V$ together with a group
   homomorphism

   $$
   \rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_K(V);
   $$

2. a $K[G]$-module homomorphism

   $$
   \varphi:M\longrightarrow M
   $$

   is a $K$-linear map also satisfying

   $$
   \varphi\circ\rho(g)=\rho(g)\circ\varphi
   $$

   for every $g\in G$.

The map $\rho$ is called a *representation* of $G$. Such representations
are often easier to handle than $G$ itself and frequently give useful
insights into $G$.

**Edition note:** the right-hand side of the source equation prints
$\rho\circ\varphi$ without the argument $g$. This edition displays
$\rho(g)\circ\varphi$, the correctly typed endomorphism expressing the
intertwining condition.
