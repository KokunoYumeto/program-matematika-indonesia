### Exercise 7.13 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w07-ex-13}

<!-- upstream_entity: Affine ebene Kurven/x^d+1 ist y^d/Parametrisierung mit Nullpunkt und Gerade x ist 1/Aufgabe -->

\qquad d\geq1.

In the following exercises, we discuss a notion of automorphism that goes beyond affine-linear changes of coordinates.

Let $K$ be a field. A polynomial map

$$
\varphi:\mathbb A_K^n\longrightarrow\mathbb A_K^n
$$

is called a **(polynomial) automorphism of affine space** if it has a polynomial inverse.

An automorphism of affine space is the same as a $K$-algebra automorphism of the polynomial ring $K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ to itself. It is given by $n$ polynomials in $n$ variables.

*Edition note.* For the stated equivalence with polynomial-ring automorphisms, the polynomial inverse must satisfy the composition identities as polynomial identities. Over a finite field, pointwise identities on $K^n$ alone do not suffice.

### Exercise 7.18 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w07-ex-18}

<!-- upstream_entity: Affiner Raum/K/Automorphismus/Jacobi-Determinante/Aufgabe -->

Let

$$
\varphi:\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^n\longrightarrow\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^n
$$

be an automorphism of affine space. Show that the Jacobian determinant of $\varphi$ is constant and equal to some $c\ne0$.

The **Jacobian problem** asks whether a polynomial map

$$
\varphi:\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^n\longrightarrow\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^n,
$$

whose Jacobian determinant is constantly equal to $1$, has a polynomial inverse, that is, is an automorphism of affine space. This problem is open even for $n=2$. By the inverse function theorem, under the given assumption there is a local differentiable inverse at every point.

Two affine algebraic sets

$$
V,\widetilde V\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n
$$

are called **affine-algebraically equivalent** if there is an automorphism of affine space

$$
\varphi:\mathbb A_K^n\longrightarrow\mathbb A_K^n
$$

such that

$$
\varphi^{-1}(V)=\widetilde V.
$$

### Exercise 7.26 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w07-ex-26}

<!-- upstream_entity: Kreis/Unterschiedliche ganzzahlige Radien/Nicht isomorph/Aufgabe -->

Let $p$ and $q$ be distinct prime numbers. Show that the quadrics defined over $\mathbb Q$,

$$
C=V\left(X^2+Y^2-p\right),
\qquad
D=V\left(X^2+Y^2-q\right)
\subset\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}^2,
$$

are not affine-linearly equivalent.

> **Edition note:** The universal claim in the source is false as written. For $p=2$ and $q=5$, the invertible rational transformation
> $$
> (x,y)\longmapsto\left(\frac{3x-y}{2},\frac{x+3y}{2}\right)
> $$
> multiplies $x^2+y^2$ by $5/2$ and therefore maps $C$ to $D$. Thus this proof exercise requires additional hypotheses.

**Source hint, with corrected ring notation.** Apply [Exercise 7.25](#br-ak-2025-2026-w07-ex-25) to

$$
R=\mathbb Q[Y]
$$

and

$$
S=(\mathbb Q[Y])[X]/\left(X^2+Y^2-p\right).
$$

> **Edition note:** The frozen hint replaces $\mathbb Q$ by $K$ without explanation and writes $X^2-Y^2+p$, which is not the coordinate equation of $C$. The notation corrected above identifies $S$ with the coordinate ring of $C$; it does not remove the counterexample to the universal claim.

### Exercise 7.32 - 6 points {#br-ak-2025-2026-w07-ex-32}

### Exercise 7.33 - 6 points {#br-ak-2025-2026-w07-ex-33}

$$
