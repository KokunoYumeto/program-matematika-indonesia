# Lecture 16: Irreducible Filters, Morphisms, and Fibres {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16}

## Irreducible filters {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-s01}

![Coffee grounds retained in a paper filter in a white ceramic holder](authority/assets/Kaffeefilter-500.jpg)

*A filter can be identified with what it retains.
Elke Wetzig (Elya), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).
Source details are given in the Unit 16 media credits.*

In the preceding lecture we saw that a point $P$ in the $K$-spectrum

$$
K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(R)
$$

determines its neighbourhood filter, and that the stalk at this filter is
the localisation of $R$ at the corresponding maximal ideal

$$
\mathfrak m_P\subseteq R.
$$

We also saw that if $R$ is an integral domain, the stalk at the filter of
all nonempty open sets gives the fraction field of $R$, which in turn is
the localisation at the zero ideal. The concept of an irreducible filter
generalises this relationship.

<!-- upstream_entity: Topologische Filter/Irreduzibler Filter/Definition -->

### Definition: irreducible filters {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-def-01}

A topological filter $F$ is called *irreducible* if

$$
\varnothing\notin F
$$

and the following condition holds: if $U,V$ are two open sets with

$$
U\cup V\in F,
$$

then $U\in F$ or $V\in F$.

For Zariski filters, that is, topological filters in the Zariski topology,
the following correspondence holds.

<!-- upstream_entity: K-Spektrum/Irreduzible Filter, Primideale, irreduzible Teilmengen/Fakt -->

### Theorem: prime ideals, irreducible closed sets, and irreducible filters {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-01}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field, let $R$ be a commutative
$K$-algebra of finite type, and let

$$
X=K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(R).
$$

The following objects correspond to one another:

1. prime ideals in $R$;
2. irreducible closed subsets of $X$;
3. irreducible filters in $X$.

An irreducible closed subset $Y\subseteq X$ corresponds to the filter

$$
F(Y)=\{U\subseteq X\mid U\text{ is open and }U\cap Y\ne\varnothing\}.
$$

The stalk of the structure sheaf at this filter is the localisation
$R_{\mathfrak p}$, where $\mathfrak p$ is the corresponding prime ideal.

**Edition note:** for the structure sheaf of algebraic functions used here,
the stalk formula presupposes that $R$ is reduced. For a general $R$,
replace it in that formula by $R/\sqrt{(0)}$. Nilpotent elements vanish as
functions. The prime ideal/filter correspondence itself is unchanged.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-01-proof}

The correspondence between prime ideals and irreducible closed subsets is
already known: a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ corresponds to the irreducible
closed subset $V(\mathfrak p)$; see Lemma 4.3 and Proposition 11.7.

The stated construction for an irreducible closed set $Y$ does indeed give
an irreducible filter. Irreducibility follows immediately from the
definition; only the intersection property of a filter needs checking. Let

$$
U,V\in F(Y),
$$

so that $Y\cap U$ and $Y\cap V$ are nonempty. Since $Y$ is irreducible,

$$
(Y\cap U)\cap(Y\cap V)=Y\cap(U\cap V)
$$

is also nonempty. Thus $U\cap V\in F(Y)$.

Now let $F$ be an irreducible topological filter. We claim that the
complement of

$$
S=\{f\in R\mid D(f)\in F\}
$$

is a prime ideal. The set $S$ is immediately a saturated multiplicative
system. It remains to show that its complement is closed under addition.
Let $g,h\in R$ with $g+h\in S$. Then $D(g+h)\in F$. Since

$$
D(g)\cup D(h)=D(g,h)\supseteq D(g+h),
$$

the set $D(g)\cup D(h)$ also belongs to $F$. Irreducibility of $F$ gives
$D(g)\in F$ or $D(h)\in F$, hence $g\in S$ or $h\in S$. Thus the
complement of $S$ is closed under addition and is a prime ideal.

Composing the three correspondences always returns the original object.
To see this, it suffices to note that an irreducible Zariski filter is
generated by open sets of the form $D(f)$; see Exercise 16.1. The assertion
about the stalk is a special case of Exercise 15.27.

The filter associated with an irreducible closed set $Y$ is also called
the *generic filter* of $Y$, and its stalk the *generic stalk* of $Y$.
The correspondence in Theorem 16.2 gives, as a special case, the
relationship between minimal prime ideals, irreducible components, and
ultrafilters. At the other extreme, maximal ideals, points, and
neighbourhood filters correspond.

## Morphisms between varieties {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-s02}

<!-- upstream_entity: Quasiaffine Varietäten/K-Spektrum/Morphismus/Definition -->

### Definition: morphisms {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-def-02}

Let $X$ and $Y$ be quasi-affine varieties, and let

$$
\psi:Y\longrightarrow X
$$

be a continuous map. We call $\psi$ a *morphism* of quasi-affine
varieties if, for every open set $U\subseteq X$ and every algebraic function

$$
f\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O),
$$

the composite function

$$
f\circ\psi:\psi^{-1}(U)\longrightarrow U
\stackrel{f}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb A_K^1
$$

belongs to $\Gamma(\psi^{-1}(U),\mathcal O)$.

<!-- upstream_entity: K-Spektrum/Morphismus/Ringhomomorphismen zu offenen Mengen/Diagramm/Bemerkung -->

### Remark: pullback homomorphisms {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-rem-01}

By definition, a morphism $\psi:Y\to X$ induces, for every open set
$U\subseteq X$, a ring homomorphism

$$
\widetilde\psi:
\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)
\longrightarrow
\Gamma(\psi^{-1}(U),\mathcal O).
$$

In particular, there is a global ring homomorphism

$$
\widetilde\psi:
\Gamma(X,\mathcal O)
\longrightarrow
\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O).
$$

If $U_1\subseteq U_2$ are open sets in $X$, we have a commutative diagram
of continuous maps

$$
\begin{matrix}
\psi^{-1}(U_1)&\longrightarrow&U_1\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
\psi^{-1}(U_2)&\longrightarrow&U_2,
\end{matrix}
$$

whose vertical arrows are open inclusions. This gives a commutative
diagram of ring homomorphisms

$$
\begin{matrix}
\Gamma(\psi^{-1}(U_1),\mathcal O)&\longleftarrow&\Gamma(U_1,\mathcal O)\\
\uparrow&&\uparrow\\
\Gamma(\psi^{-1}(U_2),\mathcal O)&\longleftarrow&\Gamma(U_2,\mathcal O).
\end{matrix}
$$

**Edition note:** the source calls $U_1,U_2$ open sets in $Y$. However,
the preceding definition, the expressions $\psi^{-1}(U_i)$, and both
diagrams require $U_i\subseteq X$. This edition displays the ambient
space determined by that context.

We collect some elementary properties of morphisms.

<!-- upstream_entity: K-Spektrum/Morphismus/Verknüpfung/Offene Einbettung/Fakt -->

### Proposition: open inclusions and composition {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-prop-01}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and let $U,X,Y,Z$ be quasi-affine
varieties. Then:

1. an open inclusion $U\subseteq X$ is a morphism;
2. if $\theta:Z\to Y$ and $\psi:Y\to X$ are morphisms, then
   $\psi\circ\theta$ is also a morphism.

The following properties are more important.

<!-- upstream_entity: K-Spektrum/Ringhomomorphismus induziert Morphismus/Fakt -->

### Theorem: algebra homomorphisms induce morphisms {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-02}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and let $R,S$ be commutative
$K$-algebras of finite type, with $K$-spectra

$$
X=K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(R),
\qquad
Y=K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(S).
$$

Every $K$-algebra homomorphism

$$
\varphi:R\longrightarrow S
$$

induces a map of spectra

$$
\varphi^*:Y\longrightarrow X
$$

that is a morphism.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-02-proof}

By Theorem 12.7, the map $\varphi^*:Y\to X$ is already known to be
continuous. Let $U\subseteq X$ be open, set

$$
V=(\varphi^*)^{-1}(U),
$$

and take an algebraic function $f:U\to K$. We must show that
$f\circ\varphi^*:V\to K$ is also algebraic. Take $P\in V$, set
$Q=\varphi^*(P)$, and choose

$$
Q\in D(H)\subseteq U,
\qquad
f=G/H\quad\text{on }D(H),
\qquad G,H\in R.
$$

By Theorem 12.7,

$$
P\in(\varphi^*)^{-1}(D(H))=D(\varphi(H)).
$$

On this open set we have

$$
f\circ\varphi^*=\frac{\varphi(G)}{\varphi(H)}.
$$

Indeed, for $\widetilde P\in D(\varphi(H))$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f\circ\varphi^*)(\widetilde P)
&=f(\varphi^*(\widetilde P))\\
&=\frac{G(\varphi^*(\widetilde P))}
        {H(\varphi^*(\widetilde P))}\\
&=\frac{(\varphi(G))(\widetilde P)}
        {(\varphi(H))(\widetilde P)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the composite is locally a rational function with nonzero
denominator, as required.

<!-- upstream_entity: K-Spektrum/Ringhomomorphismus induziert Morphismus/Auf D(F)/Bemerkung -->

### Remark: pullback on principal open sets {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-rem-02}

In the situation of Theorem 16.6, the ring homomorphism associated with
$U=D(f)$ is the natural map

$$
\Gamma(D(f),\mathcal O)\cong R_f
\longrightarrow
\Gamma((\varphi^*)^{-1}(D(f)),\mathcal O)
=\Gamma(D(\varphi(f)),\mathcal O)=S_{\varphi(f)}.
$$

**Edition note:** the identifications with these localisations use reduced
$R$ and $S$; otherwise use their reductions, as in the stalk formula above.

<!-- upstream_entity: Quasiaffine Varietät/Globale algebraische Funktion/Ist Morphismus nach affiner Geraden/Fakt -->

### Lemma: a global algebraic function is a morphism to the affine line {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-lem-01}

Let $U$ be a quasi-affine variety over an algebraically closed field $K$,
and let

$$
f\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)
$$

be an algebraic function. Then $f$ defines a morphism

$$
f:U\longrightarrow\mathbb A_K^1\cong K.
$$

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-lem-01-proof}

By Exercise 14.4, the map is continuous. Let
$U\subseteq K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(R)$, take an open set

$$
V=D(s)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^1,
\qquad
W=f^{-1}(V)\subseteq U,
$$

and an algebraic function on $V$,

$$
q=\frac r{s^n}\in\Gamma(V,\mathcal O)=K[T]_s.
$$

We need to show that $q\circ f$ is algebraic on $W$. Take $P\in W$ and a
local representation

$$
f=G/H
$$

on a neighbourhood $D(H)\ni P$. Then

$$
(q\circ f)(P)
=q(f(P))
=\frac r{s^n}\!\left(\frac GH(P)\right)
=\frac{r(G(P)/H(P))}{(s(G(P)/H(P)))^n}.
$$

The denominator is nonzero because $f(P)\in D(s)$; this is therefore the
required rational representation.

<!-- upstream_entity: Quasiaffine Varietät/Morphismus nach affiner Geraden/Fakt -->

### Theorem: morphisms to the affine line are global sections {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-03}

Let $U\subseteq K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(R)$ be a quasi-affine variety,
where $R$ is a commutative $K$-algebra of finite type over an algebraically
closed field $K$. There is a natural bijection

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Mor}(U,\mathbb A_K^1)&\longrightarrow\Gamma(U,\mathcal O),\\
\psi&\longmapsto\widetilde\psi(T),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $T$ is the variable in

$$
K[T]=\Gamma(\mathbb A_K^1,\mathcal O).
$$

In particular, a morphism from $U$ to the affine line is uniquely
determined by the global ring homomorphism

$$
\widetilde\psi:K[T]\longrightarrow\Gamma(U,\mathcal O).
$$

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-03-proof}

The displayed map is well defined and surjective. Given a global algebraic
function $f\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)$, we first have a map $f:U\to K$.
The variable $T$, which corresponds to the identity map on
$K=\mathbb A_K^1$, pulls back along $f$ to the element $f$ itself. By
Lemma 16.8, $f$ is a morphism.

Injectivity follows because both the morphism and the algebraic function
are uniquely determined by their underlying continuous map.

<!-- upstream_entity: Quasiaffine Varietät/Morphismus nach affiner Varietät/Fakt -->

### Theorem: morphisms to an affine variety as algebra homomorphisms {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-04}

Let $U\subseteq K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(R)$ be a quasi-affine variety,
where $R$ is a commutative $K$-algebra of finite type over an algebraically
closed field $K$. Let $S$ be another commutative $K$-algebra of finite
type. There is a natural bijection

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Mor}\bigl(U,K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(S)\bigr)
&\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{alg}}_K\bigl(S,\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)\bigr),\\
\psi&\longmapsto\widetilde\psi.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\widetilde\psi$ denotes the global ring homomorphism associated with
$\psi$.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-thm-04-proof}

The map is well defined. Theorem 16.9 proves the assertion for $S=K[T]$.
Since a morphism to affine space $\mathbb A_K^n$ is determined by its
components, and a $K$-algebra homomorphism from $K[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ by the
substitutions for the $T_i$, the assertion also holds for every polynomial
ring $K[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$.

Now write

$$
S=K[T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak a,
\qquad
K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(S)\cong V(\mathfrak a)=V
\subseteq\mathbb A_K^n.
$$

Composing a morphism $U\to K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(S)$ with the closed
inclusion into affine space again gives a morphism. Thus there is a
commutative diagram

$$
\begin{matrix}
\operatorname{Mor}\bigl(U,K\!-\!\operatorname{Spek}(S)\bigr)
&\longrightarrow&
\operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{alg}}_K\bigl(S,\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)\bigr)\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
\operatorname{Mor}(U,\mathbb A_K^n)
&\longrightarrow&
\operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{alg}}_K
\bigl(K[T_1,\ldots,T_n],\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)\bigr).
\end{matrix}
$$

The lower map is already known to be bijective, and both vertical maps are
injective. We need only check that the lower map identifies the two upper
subsets.

A morphism $U\to\mathbb A_K^n$ that factors through $V$ as a map is also
a morphism to $V$. It suffices to check the morphism property on principal
open sets $D(H)$ with $H\in S$. If $\widetilde H\in K[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$
represents $H$, the map

$$
K[T_1,\ldots,T_n]_{\widetilde H}\longrightarrow S_H
$$

is surjective. Thus every element of $S_H$ pulls back to an algebraic
function. On the right of the diagram, an algebra homomorphism belongs to
the upper subset exactly when $\mathfrak a$ is contained in its kernel.
The assertion now follows from Exercise 16.8.

### Example: a restriction map that is not surjective {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-exa-01}

Consider the standard cone as the closed subset

$$
V=V(X^2+Y^2-Z^2)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^3.
$$

Let

$$
U=D(X,Z-Y)\subseteq\mathbb A_K^3.
$$

The intersection $U\cap V=D(X,Z-Y)$, now regarded inside $V$, is open in
$V$. The associated ring homomorphism

$$
\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)\longrightarrow\Gamma(U\cap V,\mathcal O)
$$

is not surjective. On the left there is simply the polynomial ring in
three variables; compare Exercise 14.24. On the other hand, the equation

$$
X^2=Z^2-Y^2=(Z-Y)(Z+Y)
$$

gives an algebraic function on $U\cap V$,

$$
\frac{X}{Z-Y}=\frac{Z+Y}{X}.
$$

This function does not belong to the image of the map, because it does not
extend to a function on the whole cone.

**Edition note:** this example requires $\operatorname{char}(K)\ne2$.
In characteristic $2$ the zero locus is the plane $X+Y-Z=0$, and the
displayed function is $1$ on $U\cap V$, so it does extend. The source
does not state the characteristic restriction.

### Definition: fibres {#br-ak-2025-2026-l16-def-03}

Let

$$
\psi:Y\longrightarrow X
$$

be a morphism between affine varieties. For a point $P\in X$, the preimage

$$
\psi^{-1}(P)\subseteq Y
$$

is called the *fibre* over $P$. As a closed subset of $Y$, it is itself an
affine variety.
