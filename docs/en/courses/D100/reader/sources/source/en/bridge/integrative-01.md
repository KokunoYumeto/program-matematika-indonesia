# Integrative problem 1: from a parabola to an affine scheme {#d100-bridge-integrative-01}

This problem and its solution were independently written to connect classical
varieties with schemes; neither is a problem or answer from Holger Brenner's
source material. The prerequisites are prime ideals, localisation, and the
structure sheaf of an affine scheme. In this new explanation,
$\operatorname{Spec}$ denotes the spectrum, also written
$\operatorname{Spek}$ in the course.

## Problem {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-soal}

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field. All rings below are commutative
with identity, and all homomorphisms preserve the identity. Consider the
classical parabola and the two rings

$$
C=\{(a,b)\in k^2:b=a^2\},\qquad
A=k[x,y]/(y-x^2),\qquad
B=k[x,y]/((y-x^2)^2).
$$

Write $X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ and $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(B)$.

1. Prove that the ideal of all polynomials vanishing on $C$ is
   $(y-x^2)$. Construct an isomorphism $A\cong k[t]$ and its inverse.
2. Determine all points of $X$, the closure of each point, and the
   relationship between the classical points of $C$ and the closed points
   of $X$. What does $X$ add to the set $C$?
3. Compute the global sections, the sections on $D(x)$, the stalk at
   $p_a=(x-a,y-a^2)$, and its residue field. Also compute the stalk and
   residue field at the generic point.
4. Prove that $X$ and $Y$ have homeomorphic underlying topological spaces
   but are not isomorphic as schemes. Explain why evaluating sections at
   all points with values in their residue fields does not detect the
   difference.
5. Using $A\cong k[t]$, construct the morphism
   $F:\mathbb A^1_k=\operatorname{Spec}(k[s])\to X$ arising from the
   homomorphism $t\mapsto s^2$. Determine the image of $(s-b)$ and the
   image of the generic point. Explain the direction of the arrows between
   geometry and rings.

## Complete solution {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-jawaban}

### 1. The coordinate ring {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-a}

Division by the monic polynomial $y-x^2$, treating $y$ as the division
variable, gives

$$
f(x,y)=q(x,y)(y-x^2)+r(x),\qquad r(x)=f(x,x^2).
$$

If $f$ vanishes on $C$, then $r(a)=0$ for every $a\in k$. An algebraically
closed field is infinite: if its elements were just $a_1,\ldots,a_N$, the
polynomial $\prod_i(T-a_i)-1$ would have no root in that field, a
contradiction. A nonzero polynomial of degree $d$ has at most $d$ roots,
so $r=0$. Thus $f$ belongs to $(y-x^2)$. The reverse inclusion follows
immediately by substitution. Hence $I(C)=(y-x^2)$.

The homomorphism

$$
\alpha:A\longrightarrow k[t],\qquad
\overline x\longmapsto t,\quad \overline y\longmapsto t^2
$$

is well defined because the relation $y-x^2$ maps to zero. The
homomorphism $\beta:k[t]\to A$, $t\mapsto\overline x$, is its inverse:
$\alpha\beta(t)=t$, while $\beta\alpha$ fixes $\overline x$ and
$\overline y=\overline x^2$. From now on we use this identification and
write $x=t$ in $A$.

### 2. Closed points and the generic point {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-b}

The ring $k[t]$ is a principal ideal domain. Its prime ideals are $(0)$
and the ideals generated by irreducible polynomials. Since $k$ is
algebraically closed, those irreducible polynomials have degree one. Thus

$$
|X|=\{\eta\}\cup\{p_a:a\in k\},\qquad
\eta=(0),\quad p_a=(t-a).
$$

The ideal $p_a$ is maximal because $k[t]/(t-a)\cong k$. Consequently
$\overline{\{p_a\}}=\{p_a\}$. In contrast,
$\overline{\{\eta\}}=V(0)=X$: $\eta$ is the generic point. To see this
closure formula, observe that a closed set $V(J)$ contains a point
$\mathfrak p$ exactly when $J\subseteq\mathfrak p$; the smallest such
closed set is $V(\mathfrak p)$.

If $J\ne0$, choose a nonzero polynomial $h\in J$. The set $V(J)$ is
contained in the finite set of roots of $h$. Conversely, every finite set
$\{p_{a_1},\ldots,p_{a_r}\}$ equals $V(\prod_i(t-a_i))$. Thus the proper
closed subsets of $X$ are precisely the finite sets of closed points,
including the empty set.

The classical point $(a,a^2)$ corresponds to the kernel of evaluation
$A\to k$, namely $p_a$. This gives a bijection
$C\cong\{\text{closed points of }X\}$, and the classical Zariski topology
agrees with the subspace topology. However, $\eta$ is not a coordinate
pair in $k^2$. Through its closure property it represents the entire
irreducible parabola, rather than an arbitrarily chosen extra classical
point.

### 3. Sections, stalks, and residue fields {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-c}

For every commutative ring $R$, the structure sheaf satisfies
$\Gamma(D(f),\mathcal O)=R_f$ and
$\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}R,\mathfrak p}=R_{\mathfrak p}$.
These are [Lemma 9.12](bgk-reader.html#br-bgk-2019-l09-lem-03) and
[Lemma 9.10](bgk-reader.html#br-bgk-2019-l09-lem-02) in Brenner's course;
neither statement requires a Noetherian hypothesis. With $A=k[t]$, we obtain

$$
\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k[t],\qquad
\Gamma(D(x),\mathcal O_X)=k[t,t^{-1}],\qquad
\mathcal O_{X,p_a}=k[t]_{(t-a)}.
$$

Explicitly, elements of the last stalk can be written as $f(t)/g(t)$
with $g(a)\ne0$. Its maximal ideal is $(t-a)k[t]_{(t-a)}$, and evaluation

$$
\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\longmapsto\frac{f(a)}{g(a)}
$$

gives an isomorphism of residue fields
$\kappa(p_a)=\mathcal O_{X,p_a}/(t-a)\mathcal O_{X,p_a}\cong k$.
The stalk itself is not $k$: for example, $t-a$ is a nonzero element of
the stalk, but its class in the residue field is zero.

At the generic point, every nonzero polynomial is allowed as a denominator:

$$
\mathcal O_{X,\eta}=k(t),\qquad \kappa(\eta)=k(t).
$$

The stalk and the residue field agree here only because this local ring
is already a field. The section $1/t$ is defined on $D(x)$ but not on
all of $X$: it does not belong to $\mathcal O_{X,p_0}$.

### 4. Nilpotent structure is invisible to points alone {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-d}

Set $\varepsilon=y-x^2$. The change of variables $t=x$,
$y=t^2+\varepsilon$ gives

$$
B\cong k[t,\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
$$

Every element of $B$ has a unique expression $f(t)+\varepsilon g(t)$.
In particular, $\varepsilon\ne0$ but $\varepsilon^2=0$. Every prime ideal
$\mathfrak q$ contains $\varepsilon$: primality and
$\varepsilon^2\in\mathfrak q$ imply $\varepsilon\in\mathfrak q$.

Taking images or inverse images under
$B\twoheadrightarrow B/(\varepsilon)=A$ therefore gives a bijection of
prime ideals. This bijection respects closed sets: for every ideal
$J\subseteq B$, the set $V_B(J)$ corresponds to
$V_A((J+(\varepsilon))/(\varepsilon))$. Thus the immersion $X\to Y$
induced by this quotient is a homeomorphism on underlying topological
spaces.

However, an isomorphism of schemes would induce an isomorphism of rings
of global sections. The ring $A=k[t]$ is reduced, whereas $B$ has the
nonzero nilpotent element $\varepsilon$. This property is preserved by
ring isomorphisms. Hence $X$ and $Y$ are not isomorphic as schemes.

Every homomorphism from $B$ to a field kills $\varepsilon$, since a field
has no nonzero nilpotent elements. Thus the value of $\varepsilon$ in
every residue field is zero, although $\varepsilon$ is not the zero
section. Even at the point corresponding to $(t-a,\varepsilon)$, the
stalk $B_{(t-a,\varepsilon)}$ still contains $\varepsilon\ne0$. If a
denominator $h=f+\varepsilon g$ outside the maximal ideal annihilated
$\varepsilon$, then $h\varepsilon=f\varepsilon=0$ would force $f=0$;
yet $f(a)\ne0$. This is a contradiction. Thus stalk and sheaf data retain
infinitesimal information lost when only residue values are examined.

### 5. Contravariance in an explicit example {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-e}

By [Corollary 10.10](bgk-reader.html#br-bgk-2019-l10-cor-01), every
homomorphism of commutative rings $\theta:R\to S$ induces a morphism of
schemes $\operatorname{Spec}(S)\to\operatorname{Spec}(R)$, with point map
$\mathfrak q\mapsto\theta^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$. Take
$\theta:k[t]\to k[s]$, $t\mapsto s^2$. Then

$$
\theta^{-1}((s-b))=(t-b^2),\qquad
F(b)=(b^2,b^4)\in C.
$$

The equality of ideals follows because composition with evaluation at
$s=b$ sends $t$ to $b^2$ and has kernel $(t-b^2)$. The homomorphism
$\theta$ is injective: a nonzero polynomial $\sum c_it^i$ maps to the
nonzero polynomial $\sum c_is^{2i}$. Consequently
$\theta^{-1}((0))=(0)$, and the generic point maps to the generic point.
This argument also works in characteristic $2$; we do not claim that
the map always has two distinct preimages over each closed point.

Thus the geometric map from the line with coordinate $s$ to the parabola
pulls functions on the parabola back to functions in $s$. The ring arrow
runs in the opposite direction to the scheme arrow.

## Quick checks and pitfalls {#d100-bridge-integrative-01-check}

- $C$ corresponds to maximal ideals because $k$ is algebraically closed;
  the spectrum still uses **all** prime ideals.
- Sections, stalks, and residue fields are three different objects. A zero
  residue value is not enough to conclude that a nilpotent section is zero.
- In part 4, a homeomorphism is not an isomorphism of schemes. The structure
  sheaf is part of the object that must be preserved.
