# Lecture 10: Noetherian modules and Hilbert's Nullstellensatz {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10}

## Noetherian modules {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-s01}

We want to show that, for a Noetherian ring $R$ and a finitely generated
$R$-module, every $R$-submodule is again finitely generated. Modules with
this property are called Noetherian.

<!-- upstream_entity: Modultheorie (kommutative Algebra)/Noetherscher Modul/Definition -->

### Definition: Noetherian module {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-def-01}

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ an $R$-module. The module $M$ is
called *Noetherian* if every $R$-submodule of $M$ is finitely generated.

For $M=R$, this agrees with the definition of a Noetherian ring, since
the $R$-submodules of $R$ are precisely its ideals.

In the following statements we use the following terminology and notation.

<!-- upstream_entity: Modultheorie (kommutative Algebra)/Kurze exakte Sequenz/Definition -->

### Definition: short exact sequence {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-def-02}

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $M_1,M_2,M_3$ be $R$-modules. A diagram
of the form

$$
0\longrightarrow M_1\longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow M_3\longrightarrow0
$$

is called a *short exact sequence* of $R$-modules if $M_1$ is an
$R$-submodule of $M_2$ and $M_3$ is a quotient module of $M_2$ isomorphic to
$M_2/M_1$.

Exactness means that at every position we have

$$
\operatorname{kern}\varphi_{i+1}=\operatorname{bild}\varphi_i,
$$

where the $\varphi_i$ denote the $R$-module homomorphisms in the sequence.

<!-- upstream_entity: Modultheorie (kommutative Algebra)/Noethersche Moduln/Kurze exakte Sequenz/Äquivalentes Kriterium/Fakt -->

### Lemma: the Noetherian property in a short exact sequence {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-lem-01}

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let

$$
0\longrightarrow M_1\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M_3\longrightarrow0
$$

be a short exact sequence of $R$-modules. Then $M$ is Noetherian if and only
if both $M_1$ and $M_3$ are Noetherian.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-lem-01-proof}

First suppose that $M$ is Noetherian, and let $U\subseteq M_1$ be a
submodule. Then $U$ is also a submodule of $M$, so it is finitely generated
by assumption. Now let $V\subseteq M_3$ be a submodule of the quotient
module. Let $\widetilde V$ be the inverse image of $V$ in $M$ under the
quotient map. By assumption, $\widetilde V$ is finitely generated, and the
images of a generating set also generate the image module $V$.

Conversely, suppose that the two outer modules $M_1$ and $M_3$ are
Noetherian, and let $U\subseteq M$ be a submodule. Let $U_3\subseteq M_3$
be its image submodule. The module $U_3$ is generated by finitely many
elements $s_1,\ldots,s_n$, and we may assume that

$$
s_i=\overline r_i
$$

are the images of elements $r_i\in U$. Consider $U\cap M_1$. This is a
submodule of $M_1$, and hence finitely generated, say by $t_1,\ldots,t_k$,
which we regard as elements of $U$. We claim that

$$
r_1,\ldots,r_n,t_1,\ldots,t_k
$$

form a generating set for $U$. To see this, take an arbitrary $m\in U$. Then

$$
\overline m=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i s_i.
$$

Thus the element $m-\sum_{i=1}^n a_i r_i$ maps to $0$ on the right. It
belongs to the kernel of the quotient map, and hence to $M_1$. On the other
hand, this element also belongs to $U$, and therefore to the intersection
$M_1\cap U$, which is generated by $t_1,\ldots,t_k$. We can therefore write

$$
m-\sum_{i=1}^n a_i r_i=\sum_{j=1}^k b_jt_j,
$$

or, equivalently,

$$
m=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i r_i+\sum_{j=1}^k b_jt_j.
$$

### Theorem: finite modules over a Noetherian ring {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-01}

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and $M$ a finitely generated
$R$-module. Then $M$ is a Noetherian module.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-01-proof}

We prove the statement by induction on the number $n$ of module generators
of $M$. For $n=0$, we have the zero module. Let $n=1$. Then there is a
surjective map

$$
R\longrightarrow M\cong R/\mathfrak a.
$$

By Lemma 10.3, a quotient module of a Noetherian module is again Noetherian.
Since the ring $R$ itself is Noetherian by assumption, $M$ is also Noetherian.

Now let $n\geq2$ and suppose the statement has been proved for smaller
values. Let $m_1,\ldots,m_n$ be a generating set for $M$. Denote by $M_1$
the $R$-submodule generated by $m_1,\ldots,m_{n-1}$. This submodule gives
rise to a short exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow M_1\longrightarrow M
\longrightarrow M/M_1=:M_3\longrightarrow0.
$$

The module on the left is generated by $n-1$ elements and is Noetherian by
the induction hypothesis. The module on the right is generated by the
residue class of $m_n$, hence by one element, and is therefore also
Noetherian. By Lemma 10.3, $M$ is Noetherian as well.

## Hilbert's Nullstellensatz — algebraic version {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-s02}

For an $R$-algebra $A$, both terms *finite* and *finitely generated* will be
important. The former means that $A$, regarded as an $R$-module, is finitely
generated; the latter means that $A$ is finitely generated as an algebra.
The polynomial ring $R[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ is finitely generated in the second
sense: the variables form a finite set of algebra generators. However, the
polynomial ring is not finitely generated as a module; the simplest set of
module generators consists of all monomials.

We want to prove the algebraic version of Hilbert's Nullstellensatz. For
this we need the following two lemmas.

<!-- upstream_entity: Endlich erzeugte kommutative Algebren/R noethersch/A über R endlich erzeugt/A endlich über B/B ist endlich erzeugt/Fakt -->

### Lemma: subalgebras beneath a finite extension {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-lem-02}

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and $A$ a finitely generated
$R$-algebra. Let $B\subseteq A$ be an $R$-subalgebra over which $A$ is
finite (as a $B$-module). Then $B$ is also a finitely generated $R$-algebra.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-lem-02-proof}

We write

$$
A=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]
$$

and

$$
A=Ba_1+\cdots+Ba_m,
$$

with $a_i\in A$. *Edition supplement:* adjoin $1$ to this finite module-generating family if necessary and take $a_1=1$. This ensures that the module $\widetilde A$ constructed below contains $1$; the source's claim that it is an $S$-algebra requires this step. Set

$$
x_i=\sum_{j=1}^m b_{ij}a_j
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a_i a_j=\sum_{k=1}^m b_{ijk}a_k,
$$

with coefficients $b_{ij},b_{ijk}\in B$. Consider the $R$-subalgebra $S$ of
$B$ generated by these coefficients and the $S$-submodule

$$
\widetilde A=Sa_1+\cdots+Sa_m\subseteq A.
$$

The products $a_i a_j$ again belong to this module, so $\widetilde A$ is
even an $S$-algebra. Since all the $x_i$ also belong to $\widetilde A$, we
obtain $A=\widetilde A$. This means that $A$ is a finite $S$-module. By
Corollary 9.9, $S$ is a Noetherian ring; by Theorem 10.4, the $S$-submodule

$$
B\subseteq A
$$

is also a finite $S$-module. Finally, the chain

$$
R\subseteq S\subseteq B
$$

shows that $B$ is a finitely generated $R$-algebra.

### Lemma: the rational function field is not finitely generated {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-lem-03}

Let $K$ be a field and $R=K(X)$ the associated rational function field.
Then $R$ is not a finitely generated $K$-algebra.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-lem-03-proof}

Suppose that the rational functions

$$
F_i=\frac{P_i}{Q_i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
$$

with $P_i,Q_i\in K[X]$ and $Q_i\ne0$, form a finite generating set for
$K(X)$. By passing to a common denominator, we may assume that all the
denominators agree, namely $Q_i=Q$. In particular, the assumption says
that the rational function field could be obtained by localising at just
one element. Since $Q$ is not constant (otherwise $K[X]=K(X)$, which is
false), we have $Q-1\ne0$, and hence

$$
\frac1{Q-1}\in K(X).
$$

There is therefore a representation

$$
\frac1{Q-1}=\frac{P}{Q^s}
$$

for a suitable $s$. Consequently,

$$
Q^s=(Q-1)P.
$$

Since $Q^s$ and $Q-1$ generate the unit ideal in $K[X]$, this equality
implies that $Q-1$ alone already generates the unit ideal, so $Q-1$ is a
unit. But this would mean that $Q$ is constant, a contradiction.

The following statement is the algebraic version of Hilbert's Nullstellensatz.

### Theorem: Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, algebraic version {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-02}

Let $K$ be a field and $K\subseteq L$ a field extension that is finitely
generated as a $K$-algebra. Then $L$ is finite over $K$.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-02-proof}

Set

$$
L=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n].
$$

Let $K_i$ be the field of fractions of $K[x_1,\ldots,x_i]$ inside $L$.
Thus we have a chain of fields

$$
K=K_0\subseteq K_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq K_n=L.
$$

We want to show that $L$ is finite over $K$. By Theorem 2.8 of the course
Field and Galois Theory (Osnabrück 2018–2019), it suffices to show that
each step in this chain of fields is finite. Suppose that
$K_i\subseteq K_{i+1}$ is not finite, but all subsequent steps are finite.
Apply Lemma 10.5 to

$$
K\subseteq K_{i+1}\subset L.
$$

We obtain that $K_{i+1}$ is finitely generated over $K$. In particular,
$K_{i+1}$ is also finitely generated over $K_i$. On the other hand,
$K_{i+1}$ is the field of fractions of $K_i[x_{i+1}]$. Thus we have a chain

$$
K_i\subseteq K_i[x_{i+1}]
\subseteq Q\bigl(K_i[x_{i+1}]\bigr)=K_{i+1},
$$

where $K_{i+1}$ is finitely generated over $K_i$, but not finite. If
$x_{i+1}$ were algebraic over $K_i$, it would also be finite, and by
Exercise 10.1, $K_i[x_{i+1}]$ would already be a field. The whole of the
last chain would then be finite, contrary to the choice of $i$. Thus
$x_{i+1}$ is transcendental over $K_i$. But then $K_i[x_{i+1}]$ is
isomorphic to a polynomial ring in one variable, and $Q(K_i[x_{i+1}])$
is isomorphic to the rational function field over $K_i$. By Lemma 10.6,
this field is not finitely generated, again a contradiction.

### Theorem: inverse images of maximal ideals {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-03}

Let $K$ be a field and $A,B$ two $K$-algebras of finite type. Let

$$
\varphi:A\longrightarrow B
$$

be a $K$-algebra homomorphism. Then, for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$
of $B$, its inverse image $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak m)$ is also a maximal ideal.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-03-proof}

Let $\mathfrak m$ be a maximal ideal of $B$. From Exercise 4.19 we know
that the inverse image of a prime ideal under any ring homomorphism is
again prime. Thus $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak m)$ is initially a prime ideal;
call it $\mathfrak p$. We obtain induced ring homomorphisms

$$
K\longrightarrow A/\mathfrak p\longrightarrow B/\mathfrak m=L,
$$

where $L$ is a field and both homomorphisms are injective and of finite
type. Since the composite map is of finite type and $K,L$ are both fields,
Theorem 10.7 says that this map is finite. We want to show that the
intermediate ring $A/\mathfrak p$ is a field. This follows from Exercise 10.2.

<!-- upstream_entity: Algebren von endlichem Typ über Körper/Radikal ist Durchschnitt von maximalen Idealen/Fakt -->

### Theorem: radicals as intersections of maximal ideals {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-04}

Let $K$ be a field and $A$ a $K$-algebra of finite type. Then every radical
ideal in $A$ is an intersection of maximal ideals.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-04-proof}

By Exercise 10.17, every radical ideal is an intersection of prime ideals.
It therefore suffices to show that every prime ideal in a finitely
generated algebra is an intersection of maximal ideals. Let $\mathfrak p$
be a prime ideal and $f\notin\mathfrak p$. The ideal $\mathfrak p$ remains
a prime ideal in the localisation

$$
B:=A_f.
$$

In $A_f$ there is a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\subset A_f$ containing
$\mathfrak pA_f$. Regard $A_f$ as a finitely generated $K$-algebra and consider

$$
\varphi:A\longrightarrow A_f.
$$

We have

$$
\mathfrak p\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak m)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f\notin\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak m).
$$

By Theorem 10.8, $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak m)$ is maximal.

<!-- upstream_entity: Algebren von endlichem Typ über Körper/Algebraisch abgeschlossen/Maximale Ideale sind Punktideal/Fakt -->

### Theorem: maximal ideals are point ideals {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-05}

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and $A$ a finitely generated
$K$-algebra. Then every quotient of $A$ by a maximal ideal is isomorphic to $K$. In
other words, every maximal ideal of $A$ is a point ideal.

#### Proof {#br-ak-2025-2026-l10-thm-05-proof}

Let $\mathfrak m$ be a maximal ideal of the finitely generated $K$-algebra
$A$, and consider

$$
K\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow A/\mathfrak m=:L.
$$

Here $L$ is both a field and a finitely generated $K$-algebra. By Theorem
10.7, $L$ must be a finite $K$-algebra. Since $K$ is algebraically closed,
we must have $K=L$.
