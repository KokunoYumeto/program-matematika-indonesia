## Preparation for regularity {#br-ak-2025-2026-w08-regularity}

In the following exercises, we consider the zero locus

$$
V=V(f_1,\ldots,f_k)\subseteq\mathbb A_{\mathbb R}^n
$$

from the viewpoint of differential geometry. Recall the definition of a regular point of a differentiable map from Analysis 2. In the present context, this notion is applied to the map

$$
\varphi=(f_1,\ldots,f_k):
\mathbb A_{\mathbb R}^n\longrightarrow\mathbb A_{\mathbb R}^k
$$

at a point $P\in V$.

Let $V_0$ and $W$ be finite-dimensional real vector spaces, let $G\subseteq V_0$ be open, let $P\in G$, and let

$$
\varphi:G\longrightarrow W
$$

be differentiable at $P$. The point $P$ is called a *regular point* of $\varphi$ if

$$
\operatorname{rank}(D\varphi)_P
=\min\bigl(\dim V_0,\dim W\bigr).
$$

Otherwise, $P$ is called a *critical point* or a *singular point*.

### Exercise 8.17 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w08-ex-17}

<!-- upstream_entity: Mechanisches System/Kreis/Gerade/Disjunkt/Regularität über Jacobi-Matrix/Aufgabe -->

Consider the mechanical system given by the $x$-axis and the circle with centre $(0,2)$ and radius $1$. Let the coupling distance be $d>0$.

1. Set up equations describing this system.
2. Determine for which values of $d$ the system is regular at every point.
3. Determine the critical points as a function of $d$. How can these points be explained as a property of the mechanical system?

### Exercise 8.18 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w08-ex-18}

1. Give a geometric explanation, then prove, that for $d>3$ this mechanical system is not path-connected in the real topology.
2. Give a geometric explanation, then prove, that for $1\le d\le 3$ this mechanical system is path-connected.
