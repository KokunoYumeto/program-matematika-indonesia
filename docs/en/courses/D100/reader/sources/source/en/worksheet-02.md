### Exercise 2.11 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w02-ex-11}

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutierende Matrizen/2/Algebraische Gleichungen/Aufgabe -->

Consider the variety of commuting $2\times2$ matrices, that is, the set of
pairs of matrices

$$
V=\{(A,B)\mid A,B\in\operatorname{Mat}_2(K),\ AB=BA\}
\subseteq\operatorname{Mat}_2(K)\times\operatorname{Mat}_2(K)
\cong\mathbb A_K^8.
$$

1. Prove that $V$ is an affine variety, and find equations describing it that
   are as simple as possible.
2. Prove that the map

   $$
   \pi:V\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mat}_2(K),
   \qquad(A,B)\longmapsto A,
   $$

   is surjective.
3. Determine the inverse image of

   $$
   \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
   $$

   under $\pi$.

### Exercise 2.16 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w02-ex-16}

<!-- upstream_entity: Motzkin-Polynom/Positives Vielfaches/Darstellung als Summe von Quadraten/Aufgabe -->

Consider the polynomial

$$
F=X^4Y^2+X^2Y^4-3X^2Y^2+1.
$$

1. Find a real zero of $F$.
2. Verify the identity

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   F\cdot(X^2+Y^2+1)
   ={}&(X^2Y-Y)^2+(XY^2-X)^2+(X^2Y^2-1)^2\\
   &+\frac14(XY^3-X^3Y)^2
   +\frac34(XY^3+X^3Y-2XY)^2.
   \end{aligned}
   $$

3. Let $K=\mathbb R$. Deduce that

   $$
   F(x,y)\ge0
   $$

   for every point $(x,y)\in\mathbb A_{\mathbb R}^2$.

**Source note.** By a theorem of Artin—the solution to Hilbert's seventeenth
problem—every real polynomial that is nowhere negative can be written as a
sum of squares of rational functions. The Motzkin polynomial $F$ above gives
a concrete example showing that such a polynomial cannot in general be
written as a sum of squares of polynomials. Here, however, we prove only its
non-negativity.

### Exercise 2.20 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w02-ex-20}

<!-- upstream_entity: Ideal/Binomische Formel/Aufgabe -->

Let $I$ and $J$ be ideals in a commutative ring $R$, and let $n\in\mathbb N$.
Prove the equality

$$
(I+J)^n=I^n+I^{n-1}J+I^{n-2}J^2+\cdots+I^2J^{n-2}+IJ^{n-1}+J^n.

### Exercise 2.21 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w02-ex-21}

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutativer Ring/Restklassenring/Idealpotenzen/Aufgabe -->

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with ideals $\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b\subseteq R$.
Let

$$
S=R/\mathfrak b
$$

and let $\widetilde{\mathfrak a}=\mathfrak aS$ be the image ideal. Prove that

$$
\mathfrak a^nS=\widetilde{\mathfrak a}^{\,n}.
$$
