# Worksheet 20 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20}

## Practice exercises {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-practice}

Prove each of the following four exercises in two ways: directly and using the normality of $\mathbb Z$.

<!-- upstream_entity: Quadratwurzel/2/Irrational/Fakt/Beweis/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.1 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-01}

Show that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

<!-- upstream_entity: Quadratwurzel aus Primzahl/Irrationalität/Primfaktorzerlegung/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.2 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-02}

Let $p$ be a prime number. Using unique prime factorisation of natural numbers, show that the real number $\sqrt p$ is irrational.

<!-- upstream_entity: Primfaktorzerlegung/3 Wurzel 9/Irrational/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.3 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-03}

Use unique prime factorisation in $\mathbb Z$ to prove that $9^{1/3}$ is irrational.

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutative Ringtheorie/Z ist normal/Wurzeln aus ganzen Zahlen sind irrational/Fakt/Beweis/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.4 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-04}

Let

$$
n=p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots p_r^{\alpha_r}
$$

be the canonical prime factorisation of the natural number $n$. Let $k$ be a positive natural number, and suppose that not all exponents $\alpha_i$ are multiples of $k$. Show that the real number

$$
n^{1/k}
$$

is irrational.

<!-- upstream_entity: Normaler integrer Ring/Nenneraufnahme an einem Element ist normal/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.5 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-05}

Let $R$ be a normal integral domain and $f\in R$ with $f\ne0$. Show that the localisation $R_f$ is also normal.

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutative Ringtheorie/Normal/Nenneraufnahme ist normal/Fakt/Beweis/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.6 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-06}

Let $R$ be a normal integral domain and $S\subseteq R$ a multiplicative system. Show that the localisation $R_S$ is also normal.

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutative Ringtheorie/Normal/Durchschnitt von normalen Ringen/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.7 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-07}

Let $K$ be a field and $(R_i\subseteq K)_{i\in I}$ a family of normal subrings. Show that the intersection

$$
\bigcap_{i\in I}R_i
$$

is also normal.

<!-- upstream_entity: Integritätsbereich/Normal und Normalisierung/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.8 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-08}

Let $R$ be an integral domain. Show that $R$ is normal if and only if it equals its normalisation.

<!-- upstream_entity: Integritätsbereich/Normalisierung ist Körper/Körper/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.9 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-09}

Let $R$ be an integral domain. Suppose that its normalisation equals its field of fractions $Q(R)$. Show that $R$ itself is already a field.

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutatives Monoid/Torsionsfrei/Differenzgruppe ist torsionsfrei/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.10 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-10}

Let $M$ be a torsion-free monoid. Show that its group of differences $\Gamma(M)$ is also torsion-free.

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutatives Monoid/Torsionsfrei/Unterschiedliche Charakterisierung bei Monoid und Gruppe/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.11 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-11}

Let $M$ be a commutative group. Show that torsion-freeness of $M$ is equivalent to the following property: if $m\in M$ and

$$
rm=0
$$

for some positive natural number $r\in\mathbb N$, then $m=0$. Also show that this equivalence need not hold for a monoid.

<!-- upstream_entity: Y^2-X^4/Monoidring/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.12 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-12}

Consider the zero locus

$$
C=V(Y^2-X^4)\subseteq\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^2.
$$

1. Is $C$ irreducible?
2. Can the coordinate ring $\mathbb C[X,Y]/(Y^2-X^4)$ be obtained as a monoid ring?
3. Can the coordinate ring $\mathbb C[X,Y]/(Y^2-X^4)$ be obtained as the monoid ring of a submonoid

   $$
   M\subseteq\mathbb N\times\mathbb Z/(2)?
   $$

<!-- upstream_entity: Monoid/Einheit/Teilmenge von NxZ mod n/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.13 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-13}

Let $n\in\mathbb N_+$ and let

$$
M\subseteq\mathbb N\times\mathbb Z/(n)
$$

be a submonoid. Show that $m\in M$ is a unit if and only if it is a unit when regarded as an element of $\mathbb N\times\mathbb Z/(n)$.

<!-- upstream_entity: NxZ mod n/C/Komponenten/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.14 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-14}

Let $n\in\mathbb N_+$. Show that the $\mathbb C$-spectrum of the commutative monoid

$$
M=\mathbb N\times\mathbb Z/(n)
$$

consists of $n$ irreducible components, all isomorphic to the affine line $\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^1$.

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutative Ringtheorie/Normalisierung/Führungsideal/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.15 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-15}

Let $R$ be an integral domain with normalisation $R^{\operatorname{norm}}$. Show that

$$
\mathfrak f=
\left\{g\in R\mathrel{\Big|}gR^{\operatorname{norm}}\subseteq R\right\}
$$

is an ideal of $R$.

<!-- upstream_entity: Numerischer Monoidring/Führungsideal und Führungszahl/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.16 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-16}

Let $M\subseteq\mathbb N$ be a numerical monoid generated by relatively prime natural numbers. Show that the conductor ideal of the associated monoid ring $K[M]$ satisfies

$$
\mathfrak f=(M_{\geq f}),
$$

where $f$ denotes the conductor of the monoid.

<!-- upstream_entity: Numerisches Monoid/Singularitätsgrad/Ringkette/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.17 ★ {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-17}

Let $M\subseteq\mathbb N$ be a numerical monoid. Show that its degree of singularity equals each of the following three numbers.

1. The maximum length of a chain of monoids

   $$
   M=M_0\subset M_1\subset M_2\subset\cdots\subset M_n=\mathbb N.
   $$

2. The maximum length of a chain of $K$-algebras

   $$
   K[M]=R_0\subset R_1\subset R_2\subset\cdots\subset R_n=K[T].
   $$

3. The maximum length of a chain—a flag—of $K$-vector subspaces

   $$
   K[M]=V_0\subset V_1\subset V_2\subset\cdots\subset V_n=K[T].
   $$

<!-- upstream_entity: Kommutatives Monoid/Standardkegel/Wert der Erzeuger unter Bewertungen/Aufgabe -->
<!-- upstream_link: Monoidringe/Dimension zwei/Standardkegel/Z^2-XY/Monoid und Bewertungen/Beispiel -->

### Exercise 20.18 {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-18}

Consider [Example 20.11](#br-ak-2025-2026-l20-exa-03). What are the values of the three generators under the monoid homomorphisms $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$ to $\mathbb Z$ mentioned there, which describe the monoid? Determine the cokernel of the group homomorphism

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(M)&\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2,\\
m&\longmapsto\bigl(\varphi_1(m),\varphi_2(m)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

This cokernel is called the **divisor class group** of the monoid ring.

*Edition note on the source:* The cited example states the inequalities $t\geq0$ and $t\geq-2s$, but does not label or explicitly define $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$. Following the order of these inequalities, the intended functionals can be inferred as $\varphi_1(s,t)=t$ and $\varphi_2(s,t)=t+2s$. These formulas are an explicitly disclosed editorial inference, not an unstated assertion attributed to the source.

### Exercise 20.19 (3 points) {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-19}

Let $R$ be a normal integral domain and

$$
R\subseteq S
$$

an integral ring extension. Let $f\in R$. Show that the principal ideal generated by $f$ satisfies

$$
R\cap(f)S=(f)R.
$$

### Exercise 20.20 (6 points) {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-20}

Let $R$ be a normal integral domain. Show that the polynomial ring $R[X]$ is also normal.

<!-- upstream_entity: Normaler Integritätsbereich/X^2-a in R/in Q(R)/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.21 (5 points) {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-21}

Let $R$ be a normal integral domain and $a\in R$. Suppose that $a$ has no square root in $R$. Show that the polynomial $X^2-a$ is a prime element of $R[X]$.

*Source hint:* Use the field of fractions $Q(R)$.

*Source warning:* In this setting, being prime need not be equivalent to being irreducible.

<!-- upstream_entity: Integritätsbereich/Normal/Lokale Eigenschaft/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.22 (4 points) {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-22}

Let $R$ be an integral domain. Show that the following three properties are equivalent.

1. $R$ is normal.
2. For every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$, the localisation $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is normal.
3. For every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, the localisation $R_{\mathfrak m}$ is normal.

Normality is therefore called a **local property**.

<!-- upstream_entity: Affines Monoid/Duales Monoid/Ist normales Monoid/Aufgabe -->

### Exercise 20.23 (2 points) {#br-ak-2025-2026-w20-ex-23}

Let

$$
M\subseteq\Gamma(M)\cong\mathbb Z^n
$$

be a monoid, and consider the set

$$
M^*=
\left\{\varphi:\Gamma(M)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z
\mathrel{\Big|}\varphi(M)\subseteq\mathbb N\right\}.
$$

Show that $M^*$ is a normal submonoid of $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z^n,\mathbb Z)$.

This monoid is called the **dual monoid** of $M$.
