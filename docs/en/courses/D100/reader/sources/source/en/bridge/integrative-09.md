# Integrative problem 9: computable projective cohomology {#d100-bridge-integrative-09}

This is an independent editorial problem and solution, not an additional
public solution attributed to Holger Brenner. The calculation connects
[Theorem 26.10](bgk-reader.html#br-bgk-2019-l26-thm-01), on comparison
with Čech cohomology for the standard affine cover, and
[Theorem 27.4](bgk-reader.html#br-bgk-2019-l27-thm-02), on cohomology
of twisted structure sheaves, with evaluation of sections at a point.

## Problem statement {#d100-bridge-integrative-09-problem}

Let $k$ be any field,

$$
S=k[X_0,X_1,X_2],\qquad P=\mathbb P_k^2=\operatorname{Proj}(S),
\qquad \mathcal E=\mathcal O_P(-4)\oplus\mathcal O_P(2).
$$

For $n\in\mathbb Z$, use the description
$\Gamma(D_+(X_i),\mathcal O_P(n))=(S_{X_i})_n$;
the subscript $n$ denotes the homogeneous part of degree $n$.

1. Write down the Čech complex for the cover $U_i=D_+(X_i)$, ordered
   by $0<1<2$. Use Laurent monomials to explain why
   $H^1(P,\mathcal O_P(n))=0$ and how bases for $H^0$ and $H^2$ are
   obtained.
2. Compute all $H^q(P,\mathcal E)$ and give concrete bases for the
   nonzero groups, not merely their dimensions.
3. Take the rational point $p=[1:1:0]$. In the trivialisation on $U_0$,
   use the basis $X_0^{-4}$ for $\mathcal O_P(-4)$ and $X_0^2$ for
   $\mathcal O_P(2)$. Compute the image and kernel of evaluation
   $$
   \operatorname{ev}_p:H^0(P,\mathcal E)\longrightarrow\mathcal E(p).
   $$
   Is $\mathcal E$ generated by its global sections?

## Complete solution {#d100-bridge-integrative-09-solution}

### The Čech complex and decomposition by monomials {#d100-bridge-integrative-09-cech}

The standard affine cover and all its intersections give the complex

$$
\begin{aligned}
0\longrightarrow{}&\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2}(S_{X_i})_n
\xrightarrow{\,\delta^0\,}
(S_{X_0X_1})_n\oplus(S_{X_0X_2})_n\oplus(S_{X_1X_2})_n\\
\xrightarrow{\,\delta^1\,}{}&(S_{X_0X_1X_2})_n
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
$$

All terms are viewed inside the common Laurent ring; the restriction
maps are inclusions. With components ordered $01,02,12$,

$$
\delta^0(s_0,s_1,s_2)=(s_1-s_0,s_2-s_0,s_2-s_1),
\qquad \delta^1(c_{01},c_{02},c_{12})=c_{12}-c_{02}+c_{01}.
$$

Their composite is zero by direct cancellation. The scheme $P$ is
projective over the commutative ring $k$, and $\mathcal O_P(n)$ is
invertible, hence quasicoherent. Thus the hypotheses of Theorem 26.10
hold: the cohomology of this complex is the required sheaf cohomology.

Fix a monomial

$$
m=X_0^{\alpha_0}X_1^{\alpha_1}X_2^{\alpha_2},\qquad
\alpha_0+\alpha_1+\alpha_2=n,
$$

and write $N=\{i:\alpha_i<0\}$. This monomial occurs in
$S_{\prod_{i\in J}X_i}$ exactly when $N\subseteq J$: negative
exponents are allowed only for variables that have been inverted. The
differentials do not mix distinct monomials, so the complex decomposes
as a direct sum of coefficient complexes for individual monomials.
Every element is still a finite sum; we are not using infinite Laurent
series.

There are four possibilities.

1. If $N=\varnothing$, the coefficient complex is $k^3\to k^3\to k$
   with the maps above. The kernel of the first map consists of
   $(a,a,a)$ and has dimension one. Its image has dimension two. The
   second map is surjective with two-dimensional kernel; since
   $\delta^1\delta^0=0$, this kernel is exactly the image of the first
   map. Thus only $H^0$ contributes one copy of $k$.
2. If $\#N=1$, only one degree-zero term and two degree-one terms
   occur. The complex has the form $k\to k^2\to k$. The first map
   is injective, with each coefficient equal to $1$ or $-1$; the
   second map is surjective. Its kernel is one-dimensional and contains
   the image of the first map, so they agree. All cohomology in this
   case is zero.
3. If $\#N=2$, only one degree-one term and one degree-two term occur.
   The map between them is $1$ or $-1$, hence an isomorphism. All
   cohomology is zero.
4. If $\#N=3$, only the degree-two term occurs. This monomial contributes
   one copy of $k$ to $H^2$.

The dimension arguments hold in every characteristic: the coefficients
$1$ and $-1$ remain nonzero even when they coincide. We obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
H^0(P,\mathcal O_P(n))&=S_n,\\
H^1(P,\mathcal O_P(n))&=0,\\
H^2(P,\mathcal O_P(n))
&=\bigoplus_{\substack{\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2<0\\
                       \alpha_0+\alpha_1+\alpha_2=n}}
   k\,[X_0^{\alpha_0}X_1^{\alpha_1}X_2^{\alpha_2}].
\end{aligned}
$$

There is no cohomology in degrees $q\geq3$, since the Čech complex
ends in degree two. This is also the case $R=k,d=2$ of Theorem 27.4;
the monomial calculation exhibits the bases and exactness directly.

### Cohomology of the two twists {#d100-bridge-integrative-09-twists}

There are no polynomial monomials of degree $-4$, so
$H^0(P,\mathcal O_P(-4))=0$. For $H^2$, write $a_i=-\alpha_i$.
The conditions become $a_i\geq1$ and $a_0+a_1+a_2=4$. Their solutions
are exactly $(2,1,1),(1,2,1),(1,1,2)$. Thus a basis is

$$
\left[\frac1{X_0^2X_1X_2}\right],\qquad
\left[\frac1{X_0X_1^2X_2}\right],\qquad
\left[\frac1{X_0X_1X_2^2}\right].
$$

Square brackets denote classes in the quotient of the degree-two term
by the image of the degree-one term; these three fractions are not
global sections of $\mathcal O_P(-4)$.

For $n=2$, a basis of $S_2$ is

$$
X_0^2,\quad X_0X_1,\quad X_0X_2,\quad
X_1^2,\quad X_1X_2,\quad X_2^2.
$$

Three negative integers cannot sum to $2$, so
$H^2(P,\mathcal O_P(2))=0$. Both twists have $H^1=0$. The complex
for the direct sum $\mathcal E$ is the direct sum of the two complexes,
so its kernels, images, and cohomology also decompose. The complete
conclusion is

$$
H^q(P,\mathcal E)\cong
\begin{cases}
k^6,&q=0,\\
0,&q=1,\\
k^3,&q=2,\\
0,&q\geq3.
\end{cases}
$$

A basis for $H^0$ consists of the six pairs $(0,m)$ with $m$ in the
list of quadratics above. A basis for $H^2$ consists of the three
pairs $(\xi,0)$ with $\xi$ in the list of fraction classes above.

### Evaluation at a point and global generation {#d100-bridge-integrative-09-evaluation}

On $U_0$, use coordinates $u=X_1/X_0$ and $v=X_2/X_0$. The point $p$
is given by $(u,v)=(1,0)$ and has residue field $k$. The specified
trivialisation identifies $\mathcal E(p)$ with $k\oplus k$.

A general global section has the form $(0,f)$ with

$$
f=a_{00}X_0^2+a_{01}X_0X_1+a_{02}X_0X_2
  +a_{11}X_1^2+a_{12}X_1X_2+a_{22}X_2^2.
$$

Its local coefficient in the positive summand is
$f/X_0^2=a_{00}+a_{01}u+a_{02}v+a_{11}u^2+a_{12}uv+a_{22}v^2$.
Hence

$$
\operatorname{ev}_p(0,f)=(0,a_{00}+a_{01}+a_{11}).
$$

Its image is the line $\{0\}\oplus k$, since the section $(0,X_0^2)$
maps to $(0,1)$. Its kernel is defined by the single equation
$a_{00}+a_{01}+a_{11}=0$ and has basis

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(0,X_0X_1-X_0^2),\qquad (0,X_1^2-X_0^2),\\
&(0,X_0X_2),\qquad (0,X_1X_2),\qquad (0,X_2^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

These five vectors are linearly independent because each of the
monomials $X_0X_1$, $X_1^2$, $X_0X_2$, $X_1X_2$, and $X_2^2$
occurs in only one vector; all satisfy the kernel equation.

Since the evaluation image is one-dimensional while the fibre is
two-dimensional, global sections do not generate $\mathcal E$ at $p$.
Thus $\mathcal E$ is not globally generated. The summand
$\mathcal O_P(2)$ itself is globally generated: at any point, at least
one $X_i$ is nonzero, and the section $X_i^2$ is a generator in the
trivialisation on $D_+(X_i)$. The failure for $\mathcal E$ comes from
the summand $\mathcal O_P(-4)$, which has a one-dimensional fibre at
every point but no nonzero global sections.

## Checks and pitfalls {#d100-bridge-integrative-09-check}

The three generators of $H^2(\mathcal O_P(-4))$ all have degree $-4$
and **three** negative exponents. A fraction with any nonnegative
exponent is a coboundary in degree two. Do not confuse sheaf rank, the
dimension of the global section space, and cohomology dimensions: here
$\mathcal E$ has rank $2$, but $h^0(\mathcal E)=6$ and
$h^2(\mathcal E)=3$. Even six global sections need not generate a
two-dimensional fibre.
