Recall that in Section 1.3 we said that a tautology is a necessarily true statement, but that it doesn’t tell us anything interesting. Similarly, if a proof relied entirely on the logical form of the statement it was proving, it wouldn’t tell us anything interesting about mathematics. Thus all the proofs we consider must involve some combining of mathematical concepts in addition to their logical structure.
